## 1. Scope

S4.2 delivers the two operations that give a master their own data:

| Operation | Method and path | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| M1 `listMyAppointments` | `GET /me/appointments` | the caller's own appointments, in staff representation, filtered and paged |
| M2 `listMyServices` | `GET /me/services` | the caller's own master-service links |

Both are `JWT-M`, and both derive their scope from the token's subject rather
than from a parameter — which is REQ-F-013's AC and REQ-F-014's whole content.

### 1.1 The inventory (J1–J8)

**J1 — M1's scope is the token's subject.** The contract declares no `masterId`
parameter for M1, and REQ-F-013's AC is "the calendar shown to a logged-in master
contains only that master's appointments". A parameter would make the AC a
promise the operation could break; the subject makes it a property of the query.

**J2 — the staff representation is `AppointmentAdmin`.** It is the client view
minus `accessToken` and `confirmationUrl` (D18, REQ-NF-006): the master may see
the client's name and phone, because they need them to deliver the service, but
not the token that lets anyone read the record without authenticating.
`payment` and `notificationsSummary` are optional in the contract and are not
produced here — S5.3 and S5.1 are the stages that have something to put in them.

**J3 — `from` and `to` filter on `startAt`, inclusive of the whole day.** The
calendar's axis is when the appointment starts. M7's `from`/`to` filter on
`createdAt` because a notification's own date is when it was written; the two
operations answer different questions and must not share a column.

**J4 — the order is `startAt` then `id`.** A calendar reads forwards, and two
appointments can start at the same instant, so the secondary key keeps a page
reproducible.

**J5 — M1 dispatches a due reminder.** H3 says a due row becomes `sent` on the
next read, and M1 is a read of appointments. A calendar that showed a
`scheduled` reminder whose moment had passed would be the one read that did not.

**J6 — M1 is `JWT-M`.** The contract declares 403, and the administrator's own
calendar is R20's (S5.1), not this operation's.

**J7 — M2 returns the caller's links unpaged, in `createdAt` then `id` order.**
`MasterServiceList` is a bare array with no page envelope, and the order is R10's,
so the two lists agree.

**J8 — M2 is `JWT-M`**, for the same reason as J6.

### 1.2 What the stage does not deliver

Nothing is carried forward. The administrator's calendar (R20) and the
administrator's link list (R10, already delivered) are separate operations, and
`payment`/`notificationsSummary` are optional fields whose stages are named in
J2.

### 1.3 Shared changes this stage made

Three changes reach beyond the two operations. They were not visible when §5 was
written (it was written before the code), and they are recorded here rather than
left in the diff:

1. **`src/middleware/authorize.ts` gains `masterAccess`.** S1.3 wrote the pair
   `[authenticate, requireRole('master')]` out inside
   `availability/routes.ts`; S4.2 is the third module to need it. It is now
   declared once beside `adminAccess`, and `availability/routes.ts` spreads it
   instead of its local copy. The gate's §5 item 3 and 4 name the two new
   call sites; this one is the third.
2. **`LinkListFilter.limit` and `.offset` become optional** (§5 item 5). R10
   passes both and is unchanged; M2 passes neither. Drizzle's builder takes a
   number, not `undefined`, so `listLinks` builds the query in two branches —
   with a page and without. The filter is otherwise the same code, which is what
   keeps J7's "R10's order" true by construction rather than by coincidence.
3. **`tests/manifest.json` gains one entry**, `SMS-31 (test) → SMS-31
   (implementation)`, with one `implementation` path (`staff-view.ts`). The
   other five files the stage touches predate the test commit and are listed as
   `touches`; §4 states what the checker can and cannot prove about them.

None of the three changes an operation's contract. (1) moves a gate, (2) widens
a parameter, (3) records an ordering.

## 5. What GREEN implements

1. **`src/modules/appointments/staff-view.ts`** — `staffBody(record)`: the
   `AppointmentAdmin` representation, which is `appointmentBody` without the two
   client-only fields (J2). A separate module because the exclusion is a security
   rule (D18), not a formatting choice.
2. **`src/modules/appointments/service.ts`** — `listMyAppointments(masterId,
   filters, limit, offset)`: the page, the total, the lines and the reminders,
   with the dispatch of J5 in front of the read.
3. **`src/modules/appointments/routes.ts`** — M1, behind `masterAccess`, with
   `validateParams('listMyAppointments')`.
4. **`src/modules/master-services/routes.ts`** — M2, behind `masterAccess`,
   answering `MasterServiceList` (a bare array).
5. **`src/modules/master-services/service.ts`** — `LinkListFilter.limit` and
   `.offset` become optional, so M2 can ask for the caller's links without a page
   envelope. R10's call is unchanged.
6. **`src/modules/notifications/service.ts`** — `readRemindersFor(executor, ids)`,
   the batch form of `readReminders`, so the calendar loads a page's reminders in
   one query rather than one per appointment.
